## Recommendation and decision

**Include Shen and Tamkin's source as bounded evidence about immediate individual
skill acquisition.** Its contribution is material: a workflow can help a person
complete an unfamiliar programming task while leaving less independently
demonstrated understanding immediately afterward. Learning therefore belongs
beside completion and time in the Code outcome inventory. The proposed inference
concerns that measured boundary; durable competence and collective ownership
remain separate questions.

**Approve the concrete text and dispositions below:** a short Chapter 5 section;
one register source card; a dated HUM-1 assessment of **Provisional support for the
immediate individual-learning component**, with durable/shared capability and the
remedy still **Open**; Code and Review coverage updates without changing their
existing verdicts; and README evidence navigation. Preserve the study's completion
advantage, inconclusive mean time comparison, measurement/reporting limits and
exploratory prompting patterns.

This approval would authorize implementing that treatment, necessary corrections,
independent integration review, required checks, merge, source verification and
actual-main acceptance within #4. It would not select #100, implement #92/#93 in
full, approve a pilot, or change governance or production controls.

## Exact source and decision-relevant findings

Shen and Tamkin, [*How AI Impacts Skill Formation*, arXiv:2601.20245v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20245v2),
submitted **1 February 2026**; v1 submitted 28 January 2026. The reviewed object is
the v2 preprint, not an assumed final peer-reviewed edition. The
[source brief](../../evidence/primary/2026-ai-impacts-skill-formation.md) owns the
complete acquisition, design, disclosure, exclusion and source-review record.
Section and figure locators below refer to that exact original.

| Finding or design boundary | Original locator | Proposed use and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main study analyzes 52 participants, 26 per arm, after 58 invited, 53 completed and one exclusion; 48 of the analyzed 52 report at least four years of coding experience, while all are new to Trio | §5.2.1 and Table 1 | Describe developers learning an unfamiliar library. Do not call the sample 52 programming beginners, infer a universal seniority effect, or replace invitation with analysis counts |
| GPT-4o chat assistance versus instructions/examples and permitted web resources | §§4.1, 4.3; Appendix D, Figure 23 | Compare the actual workflows, not a fully autonomous coding agent against an idealized unaided engineer |
| Immediate unassisted assessment: 14 questions, 27 possible points; AI arm scores lower, reported Cohen's d = .738, p = .010; covariate-adjusted result d = .725, p = .016 | §4.2, §5.2.2 and Figure 6 | Evidence about immediate independent performance after the learning tasks. These statistics do not measure long-term retention, prior-skill loss or collective ownership |
| Mean completion-time comparison is not statistically significant, p = .391 | Figure 6 | Do not claim established average acceleration, zero time effect or equivalence from this test |
| Task 2 was completed within 35 minutes by 26/26 AI-arm participants and 22/26 controls | §5.2.2 | Preserve the observed completion advantage, including the four unfinished control tasks; do not turn these counts into a measured production-throughput effect |
| Median encountered task errors: 1 in the AI arm, 3 in control; IQRs 0–3 and 2–5 | §6.2, Table 4 | A task-interaction observation. It is not a production defect rate, escaped-error rate, or independently identified learning mechanism |
| Six prompting/interaction patterns and assessment-subarea analyses are exploratory | §6, Figure 11, and §5.2.2; query categories in §6.1, Table 3 | Preserve potentially useful variation. Self-selected patterns are not randomized remedies and do not prove that a particular prompting strategy preserves capability |

**Numerical discrepancy:** the reported raw score gap of **4.15 points out of 27**
normalizes to `4.15 / 27 × 100 = 15.37 percentage points`. The paper also describes
the gap as **17%**; these are not transparently the same quantity. Do not silently
substitute a denominator, relabel percentage points as percent, or reproduce 17%
as an independently checked headline. The proposed report/register use the
reported standardized effect and p-value; the brief and
[numeric record](issue-4-2026-10-22-numeric-checks.json) retain the raw-point
discrepancy. The arithmetic result is not promoted to a newly verified observed
effect. Standardized d is not a percentage loss of skill.

Random assignment supports the bounded treatment comparison, subject to the
analyzed sample, exclusions, assessment and implementation described by the
authors. Selection, small sample, short novel-library tasks, one chat-tool
condition, immediate testing and incomplete long-horizon coverage limit
generalization. The time result is uncertain; the completion and learning results
are different endpoints. Video/recording coverage and public-data availability
must retain the appendix ambiguities recorded in the brief. No raw-data
replication or reconstruction of unpublished observations is claimed. The
preregistration was inaccessible, and the inspected public materials do not
supply participant quiz scores or an exclusion map. Their condition/recording
counts do not transparently reconstruct the analyzed 26 + 26 groups. Those gaps
remain unresolved; the review does not independently certify preregistration
compliance. The preprint's Anthropic relationships and GPT-4o treatment are
disclosed in the brief; an inaccessible OpenReview lead does not establish
accepted publication or equivalence to another edition.

**Source interpretation versus repository inference:** the authors discuss
cognitive engagement and patterns of AI use as possible explanations. The
repository may infer that the learning function deserves explicit measurement
alongside task completion. The proposed integration does not identify reduced
practice as the causal mediator, prescribe a proven learning strategy, or equate
an immediate quiz with an expert team's system model.

## Complete node outcomes

The current register already separates task output, learning, maintainable
handoff and feedback in Code; quality, ownership, knowledge transfer and
coordination in Review. HUM-1 calls for distinct individual, expert and collective
capability observations. Its current mechanism and remedy verdicts are Open.
The #86/#87 assessments remain dated history; this proposal adds a narrowly
defined successor without replacing the earlier source results.

The actual study baseline is access to instructions/examples and permitted web
resources while learning a new library. Control performance demonstrates only
the measured task and immediate assessment outcomes in that setting. It does not
prove that every previous SDLC workflow delivered all intended functions well.

| Node function/outcome | Actual baseline evidence | Source coverage, proposed mechanism and remaining gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Code: correct executable behavior and implementation feedback | Study-task completion and encountered errors, with four control participants not finishing Task 2 within the limit | AI-arm completion is higher for that task; mean time evidence is inconclusive. Laboratory completion/errors do not establish production correctness, usefulness or feedback to a real product owner |
| Code: implementation/debugging learning and visible assumptions | Immediate unassisted quiz performance after the same learning tasks; no delayed assessment | Lower AI-arm immediate scores directly inform individual skill acquisition. Chat assistance may displace or supply learning; the proposed mediator and durable effect are not independently identified |
| Code: understandable, ownable and modifiable handoff | No receiving-maintainer or team handoff assessment | Individual post-task performance is adjacent to maintainability capability; no measured transfer to another maintainer, shared ownership, later change success or tool dependence |
| Code: feedback to selection/design, including abandoned work | Task completion under a time limit; no product-selection or avoided-work accounting | No measured value of exploration, downstream decision quality, or total effort for successful and abandoned product paths |
| Review: quality gate | Quiz/task observations rather than production code-review decisions | The study does not measure actual review detection, escaped errors, security, severity or false alarms. A debugging/assessment result is not a production quality-gate effectiveness estimate |
| Review: ownership gate and shared understanding | Individual immediate assessment; no accountable receiving team or shared-system test | No demonstrated distribution of knowledge beyond the producer, responsibility acceptance, or diagnosis/change/containment/recovery of a maintained system |
| Review: knowledge transfer, context and learning | Immediate individual learning is measured; mentoring, domain transfer and delayed retention are not | Supplies a direct learning endpoint relevant to this intended function, without testing team review as a learning mechanism or a knowledge-transfer substitute |
| Review: admission, coordination and handoff | No review queue, service-demand or capacity baseline | No review-path cost, active reviewer hours, interruptions, quality-adjusted headroom or handoff failure estimate |

**HUM-1:** the source adds an immediate individual-learning observation to an
inventory that also requires durable learning, a shared system model, knowledge
beyond one producer and capability during tool/author absence. Those later and
collective outcomes remain unmeasured. Assessment without the assistant is not a
production fallback test for an expert team.

**Symmetric tests and tradeoffs:** preserved or improved independent understanding
after comparable delegation, with adequate precision and observation, would
weaken the adverse learning prediction in that setting. A predefined learning
substitute must be compared with the actual workflow; no durable capability gain
worth its learner/mentor/tooling costs would weaken that remedy. The six observed
interaction patterns suggest candidate practices, not demonstrated interventions.
Do not choose a favorable post-hoc pattern and call the remedy validated.

Technical and learning owners would have to decide the acceptable completion,
time and capability tradeoff for their own work, preserving justified quality and
recovery floors. This source supplies no universal margin or consent on their
behalf. The proposed downstream link is that sufficient understanding may support
safe change, recovery and useful service; user benefit, competitive advantage,
burnout, debt and full lifecycle economics still require their own measurements.

```markdown
### Skill Formation Is a Separate Outcome

Completing a task and learning enough to work independently afterward are
different outcomes. In [Shen and Tamkin's randomized study](../evidence/primary/2026-ai-impacts-skill-formation.md),
52 analyzed developers learned the unfamiliar Python library Trio, with GPT-4o
chat assistance or a comparison workflow using instructions, examples and web
resources. Most had substantial coding experience: 48 reported at least four
years. All were new to this library.

The AI arm scored lower on an immediate unassisted 14-question assessment
(reported Cohen's d = .738, p = .010; adjusted analysis retained the result).
The mean completion-time comparison was inconclusive (p = .391), while all 26
AI-arm participants and 22 of 26 controls finished the second task within the
study's 35-minute programming limit. This preserves a useful completion result alongside the
learning difference. The brief documents exclusions, the paper's inconsistent
percentage summary and the limits of its exploratory interaction patterns.

**Repository inference:** learning is part of what the coding workflow supplies,
so a completion metric can miss a consequential outcome. This study measures
immediate individual performance; it does not establish durable skill loss,
expert system understanding or collective ownership. Its six observed prompting
patterns do not prove a remedy. [HUM-1](appendices/mechanism-hypotheses.md#hum-1--practice-understanding-and-collective-ownership)
keeps those questions separate, and the [Review inventory](appendices/mechanism-hypotheses.md#review--rev-1-and-rev-2)
retains both quality and competent ownership as outcomes to evaluate.
```
