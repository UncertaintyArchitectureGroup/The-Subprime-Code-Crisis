## Human decision and adequacy analysis

The central thesis, H0/node cards and existing procedures already name alternatives,
weakening observations and counterevidence. Their missing step is a consistent,
inspectable comparison of the observations predicted by applicable explanations.
A compatible finding may support a shared premise without identifying a mechanism;
it may also favor one compatible explanation through a more specific predicted
pattern. The method must distinguish those cases rather than treating all shared
compatibility as equally informative or automatically non-discriminating.

Alternatives were to preserve scattered alternatives unchanged, add a register
without a shared assessment contract, or extend the existing method and flows.
The approved third option adds a compact contract in existing records and a
single canonical register section. It requires relevance screening and justified
comparison, not six artificial judgments for every study, a citation quota,
numerical probabilities, a new registry/Flow, a new approval gate or a favorable
result. The change makes contributor expectations explicit without claiming new
empirical support for the thesis or any rival.

The six families cover adequate capability/functional substitution, transitional
adaptation, task mix/measurement, demand/adoption, selection/heterogeneity and
net/remedy value. ALT-6a and ALT-6b are separately judged propositions. Net benefit
can coexist with a measured adverse learning or maintenance endpoint; remedy
value requires the specified intervention's incremental comparison. All preserve
critical outcome floors, Review quality and competent ownership, an actual
imperfect baseline, bounded horizons and observations that could weaken them.
