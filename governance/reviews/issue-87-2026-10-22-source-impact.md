## Exact original and source-level result

Chen and Stratton, *Artificial Intelligence in the Firm: Bottlenecks in Software
Production*, **4 August 2026**, 78-page Job Market Paper. Canonical
[PDF](https://fion.ac/jellyfish.pdf), SHA256
`27f8d1fe0a2c145e700ae8835c997533fb4483e21a11f9c2385186bdb9f7c096`.
Public manuscript; proprietary Jellyfish records/code not acquired. No journal
acceptance or raw-data replication is claimed. No private correspondence is used.

The full brief owns exact methods, figures, disclosures and calculations. The
decision-relevant observations are:

- Firm-month observational estimates after agent adoption show approximately
  **29.8% more added lines and 22.8% more submitted PRs per active worker-month**
  (Figure 6, p39). These are activity rates, not measured time savings.
- Resolved Jira issues: **+0.12 (SE 0.17)** from 3.67, approximately **+3.3%**;
  a normal interval from rounded numbers is about **−5.8% to +12.4%** (Figure 7,
  p40). Moderate benefits remain compatible with this uncertain estimate.
- PR submission-to-merge elapsed time: **+3.45 days (SE 1.60)** from 7.03,
  approximately **+49.1%** (Figure 11, p44). This is not active review labor.
- Formal change requests, comments and reviewer participation increase in the
  agent specification (Figures 12–13, pp45–46). They indicate process changes,
  not a directly measured bug rate or loss of team understanding.
- The earlier assistant estimates do not show significant worsening in those
  latency/change-request/comment measures; positive assistant commits and
  increased reviewer participation remain visible.

These jointly observed estimates are informative. Their difference is not a
jointly tested pass-through coefficient. Visible pre-period movements, selection
and adoption assumptions constrain causal attribution. Jira resolution is a
tracked-completion proxy; a merge is not user benefit. Main/appendix discrepancies
affect interview counts and auxiliary task-size labels/scales. They are documented
without repairing the source by assumption.

**Source-author interpretation:** code-review bottlenecks and a model-based
bug-rate channel limit gains. **Proposed repository inference:** the observations
strengthen the reason to evaluate complementary SDLC work and its constraints;
they do not identify the entire chain or establish that AI-review assistance
fails. Increased reviewer participation is evidence that some adaptation occurs;
whether it is adequate or economical is not measured.

## Whole-node outcome coverage

The register's intended functions are the assessment inventory. Baseline counts
establish activity, not that the old process reliably delivered every function.
The source is an observational comparison of actual adoption timing, not a test
of our proposed rebalancing intervention. Follow-up differs by tool generation;
there is no delayed independent capability/recovery assessment.

| Node function | Actual baseline evidence | What this source adds or leaves missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Code: correct executable behavior and implementation feedback | Activity rates; correctness unmeasured | More code/commits/PRs; no directly audited semantic correctness or accepted useful behavior |
| Code: implementation/debugging learning | Unmeasured | Neither retained understanding nor displaced practice tested |
| Code: maintainable, understandable handoff | PR metadata; recipient comprehension unmeasured | Review-path measures are relevant proxies, not a handoff-capability assessment |
| Code: feasibility/defect/unused-work feedback | Unmeasured | No value accounting for experiments, rejected variants or avoided future work |

| Review: learning, domain context and standards | Unmeasured | Comment counts do not establish learning; changed standards remain an alternative explanation |
| Review: admission, coordination and handoff | Submission-to-merge latency and participation baselines | Longer elapsed path and more reviewer participation; independent service demand, active hours, headroom, interruptions and total cost missing |
| Integration: compose components and reconcile interfaces | Unmeasured | Merge timing does not isolate interface reconciliation or semantic integration failures |
| Integration: emergent behavior and coherent baseline | Unmeasured | No system-level assurance, escaped cross-component failures or rebuild cost |
| Integration: knowledgeable cross-team coordination | Unmeasured | No dependency-specific active work, cross-boundary understanding or ownership test |
| Integration: release-ready handoff and feedback | Jira/PR completion proxies | No audited coherent delivery, complete risk handoff or demonstrated user result |

Intent, requirements, design, testing, deployment, operation, realized user
outcomes and economics are not assessed end-to-end by this study. Its qualitative
workflow discussion does not fill those quantitative gaps. Burnout and debt
remain separate hypotheses. A higher cost could be justified by greater benefit;
no net benefit/harm result is obtained without that comparison.

**Symmetric tests and tradeoffs:** H0-B/REV-1 still require an independently
measured increase in admitted service demand relative to competent capability,
not a trigger inferred from the same delay being explained. Adequate unchanged
practice with preserved quality/ownership and no added burden under that trigger
would weaken the proposed mechanism. Higher participation could be successful
adaptation; longer time could reflect more useful scrutiny, scheduling or other
dependencies. These alternatives must remain testable.

REV-2 and H0-R need a specified assistance/admission/review intervention against
actual practice, counting setup, false alarms, displaced work and later recovery.
No meaningful net gain, critical capability loss or greater burden without
compensating benefit would weaken that remedy. Review/technical owners and the
receiving team must set context-appropriate quality/capability floors and accept
tradeoffs; this paper supplies no universal thresholds. These are retrospective
evidence assessments and future test requirements, not a preregistered experiment.
