## Full-outcome assessment of the bounded protocol change

This applies the [#85 method](../hypothesis-evaluation.md). The baseline is the
documented protocol, not evidence that any team already performed it well.
Actual historical delivery of the functions below is **unmeasured** in this
repository; no pilot or empirical improvement verdict is assigned.

| Function/outcome and affected node | Documented baseline and actual evidence | Change, owner or functional substitute | Observable comparison, possible gain and tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intended transformation and acceptance; requirements/design | Engineer was to understand outputs and failure modes; actual practice unmeasured | The responsible engineer distinguishes isolated rehearsal from accepted persistent effects and defines intended data/operational changes | Compare correctly applied intended transformations and missed requirements per attempted change, including rejected/failed runs; extra preparation may prevent errors but consumes time |
| Correctness, integrity and assurance; code/review/test | Generated output required verification before durable code; data-side interpretation was ambiguous | Existing risk owner applies data/operational class before execution; suitable independent checks or automation may supply assurance | Compare escaped errors, integrity/security effects and total checking effort for matched changes; isolated speed gains alone cannot establish whole-node improvement |
| Understanding, learning and competent shared ownership; review | Understanding and human acceptance were intended, but a name or approval was not measured capability | Accepted durable effects have an owner able to explain the transformation, limits and failure response; reusable verified tooling may substitute for manual inspection | Assess explanation, diagnosis, change/recovery ability and dependence on one person/tool; record transfer/learning time. No added gate click proves capability and no loss of manual reading proves harm |
| Handoff, dependencies and decision rights; integration/deployment | Team risk classes already existed; disposable-script label could bypass their intended scope | P1/P2 consistently route persistent-data changes through existing High/Critical classes as effects require, before execution | Observe unowned changes, missed dependencies, handoff delay and total effort per attempted/accepted change; avoid duplicate approval where existing controls already supply the function |
| Failure containment, recovery and future change; operation | Stopping/discarding a script does not itself undo its effects; actual recovery evidence unmeasured | Owner accepts recovery limits before running; irreversible effects are explicitly considered | Compare recovery success, data loss, service/user impact and support effort over a locally justified follow-up; backup/rehearsal cost is part of the comparison |
| Coordination, feedback and useful delivery; admission across nodes | P4 §1 used admitted demand, §5 candidate wording diverged | Diagram/prose now refer to work actually admitted to that downstream stage, its size/risk/familiarity and assistance | Compare admitted service demand, capacity, waits/WIP, completed useful outcomes and quality; effective assistance or sufficient headroom may make extra control unnecessary |

The harm prediction is that accepting persistent effects as disposable may omit
needed understanding, assurance or recovery work. The remedy prediction is that
explicit effects-based classification preserves those outcomes at acceptable
total burden. Both remain testable: matched unchanged practice with sufficient
outcomes weakens necessity; classification adding delay/cost without a meaningful
outcome gain weakens the remedy. Required quality/capability margins, risk
acceptance and observation horizon belong to the local decision owner; this
change supplies no universal threshold. Missing measurement is neither harm nor
preservation. Useful user outcomes, avoided harm and total lifecycle cost remain
the final comparison; no competitive advantage or ROI is asserted by this edit.
