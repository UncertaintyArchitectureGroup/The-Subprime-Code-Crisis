## Thesis and alternatives

Working hypothesis: in a defined setting, admitted durable change may grow faster than effective comprehension and verification capacity, with a measurable penalty for later maintenance or recovery. Better code-generation quality alone does not establish that this capacity has scaled.

Alternative explanations and counterexamples must remain: automated verification can reduce residual effort; good abstractions can reduce comprehension demand; disposable experiments may never acquire durable obligations; improved tools may support sustained net acceleration. Candidate generation is not equivalent to admission, raw line count is not semantic load, and human-only line-by-line review is not the definition of control.

Rejected formulations: inevitable collapse; 100% proof; a universal law of conserved complexity; all agent review is useless; every line must be understood by a human; no substantial speedup is possible; an industry-wide fixed human capacity; a universal 20–30% productivity ceiling; deliberate media suppression without evidence.

## Argument and section roles

1. Better code: remove low generation quality as a necessary premise.
2. Understanding: define an observable capacity for consequential intervention, not omniscience.
3. Admission: separate speculative output from maintained responsibility.
4. Capacity: expose assumptions and units; distinguish an accounting identity from a falsifiable empirical hypothesis.
5. Speed and control: present a third path through abstraction, independent assurance and bounded change.
6. Agent review: distinguish useful additional evidence from shared assumptions and mere agreement.
7. Maintenance: include state, diagnosis and recovery rather than only initial acceptance.
8. Testing: define outcomes, delayed tasks, confounders and disconfirming observations.
9. Response: proposed practices and a bounded conclusion, not new mandatory protocols.

## Research questions and next decisions

R1: Can adequate understanding be measured through unfamiliar intervention tasks without reducing it to recall or self-report?
R2: Under which architectures does automation reduce residual verification burden faster than admitted workload grows?
R3: What kind of review diversity provides materially independent evidence?
R4: What level of recovery independence is warranted at different consequence and reversibility levels?
