## Identity, question and population

The cover identifies January 2025/Q4. Methods pp 7/45: July–September 2024 fieldwork;
2,773 senior leaders across 14 countries and six industries, split equally between
IT and business leaders. Organizations already used AI daily and had GenAI pilots
or daily implementations; respondents influenced/managed AI decisions. Fifteen
executive interviews supplement the survey. This is a selected AI-active sample,
not representative uptake among all enterprises; anonymous case studies are not
independently verified effect estimates.

The retired S-2025-01 moving series URL now leads to a 2026 page. No historical
Q3 2025 edition was recovered. This is an **explicit identified replacement**,
not a claim to have discovered which edition the old author intended.

## Findings and calculations

- **p 22 / Figure 6:** for **one of the organization's most advanced/scaled GenAI
  initiatives**, 43% report ROI meeting expectations and 31% exceeding them;
  `43+31=74%`. This is the share of answers about expectations, not 74% ROI,
  measured net profit, all projects or enterprise-wide success.
- **p 18 / Figure 3:** over two-thirds anticipate fully scaling 30% or fewer of
  current experiments within the next 3–6 months. This is a forecast of near-term
  scaling, not an observed 70% failure rate.
- Positive returns coexist with integration/regulatory/data/workforce constraints
  and differing function-level results (pp 14/19–23/25–26). The report includes
  positive cybersecurity and sales cases as well as unmet expectations.

No pooled effect across McKinsey/Deloitte, causal comparison, raw-data replication
or uncertainty interval is supplied here. Rounded categories need not total 100%.

## Interpretation and limits

**Authors:** positive pragmatism, sustained commitment and organizational work
alongside early returns. Their recommendations are consulting interpretation,
not an experimental effect of a mandatory transformation programme.

**Repository:** use alongside McKinsey in R1 Ch 3 to distinguish use, benefit,
selection and scaling. The instrument does not measure investor fear, a failed
adopt-first strategy, a review-time multiplier or the value of all coding-tool
adoptions. Consulting interests, self-report, advanced-initiative selection and
short-horizon expectations limit generalization. No independent audit of ROI
calculation or the anonymous firms is claimed.
